    0.32.3: With ND Dam Hazard 2.34.3 and ND Dam Breach Runner 3.26.0. A dam screened on its own and then modelled from the Runner window no longer passes the normal-pool (mapped water) area to the Runner as the area at the top of dam. The Runner turns that area into its storage-area exponent (area x height / storage); on a dry flood-control dam the mapped water area is near zero, which forced the exponent to its floor of 1 and made the peak breach flow about a third too low (Absaraka DWR-2801: 19,935 vs 28,671 cfs). The tool-7b / runchain hand-off already left it out for this reason; tool 8 (the dam record it writes to dam.json) now does the same, so the Runner uses its depth-squared default (exponent 2) when no top-of-dam area is known. No step, default or number changes otherwise.

    0.29.0: With ND Dam Hazard 2.31.0, ND Dam Breach Runner 3.21.0 and ND Data Updater 1.5.0 (damsuite 1.17.0). Two fixes that made steps fail on real data. (1) 'Merge dams and ponds' (step 18) and the Review tab's 'Move breach point' now work on a GeoPackage that QGIS wrote. Both write with plain Python sqlite3, and a QGIS-written GeoPackage carries a spatial index whose triggers call a function (ST_IsEmpty) that plain sqlite3 does not have, so the merge stopped with 'no such function: ST_IsEmpty' and the move logged a failure and left the point where it was. The spatial index on that layer is removed before the write (QGIS rebuilds one if it needs it); the data is untouched. (2) 'Download the terrain for a county' now covers the whole county. The county extents were hand-typed and ended inside 48 of the 53 counties (McLean by about 34 miles on the west), so a county DEM download quietly stopped short and dams in the missing strip had no terrain; the extents are now taken from the county outlines the suite ships. No screening number moves.
    0.28.3: With ND Dam Hazard 2.30.3, ND Dam Breach Runner 3.20.3 and ND Data Updater 1.4.3 (damsuite 1.16.3). Three fixes from the ND vs DamSafety comparison of Absaraka Dam. (1) THIS MOVES THE PEAK BREACH FLOW OF DRY DAMS. Tool 3: a basin found in the DEM (flat water or a closed depression, not a mapped water body) that is less than half the dam height deep is not the reservoir - it is a puddle on the floor of a dry basin - so the full-height assumed curve from the inventory storage and height is used instead of stretching the shallow one to the DWR maximum. At Absaraka the stretched curve put all 1,615 ac-ft into the top 4 ft of a 27.5 ft dam, which gave a surface area of 383 ac against the inventory's 155 and a storage-area exponent at the Runner's cap. Dams whose storage came from a mapped water body, or from a DEM basin as deep as the dam, do not change. (2) The hand-off sends no surface area to the Runner when the stage-storage curve spans much less than the dam height, so the Runner assumes storage varies with depth squared and says so, instead of reading an exponent off a curve that does not describe the reservoir. (3) Tool 15 (the headless chain) and the Runner hand-off now FAIL when the run fails, so qgis_process exits non-zero and a script or a scheduled job notices; before, a chain stopped by the preflight and a batch in which every dam failed both ended with exit code 0. FAIL_ON_ERROR=false restores the old behaviour, and the hand-off also reports DONE / FAILED / SKIPPED counts. The settings a headless run is given may now name the suite data folder ("suite_folder"), for a computer with no QGIS setting.
