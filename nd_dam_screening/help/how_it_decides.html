How the screening decides

ND Dam Breach Screening 0.13. Pictures of the rules the tools apply, with the parameter each line of the picture belongs to. Every value marked YOUR value is a screening threshold the engineer chooses; the suite has no standard for it. The official hazard classification is the North Dakota Department of Water Resources'.

1. Ponds and dugouts: the rim, the toe and the head (step 16)

The LiDAR is the only information the tool has about a pond. The ground around the water is cut into 16 compass sectors; in each, the ground profile away from the shore is read in bands of two metres, and the embankment face is the longest run of bands that keeps falling steeper than the face angle. Its top is the rim, its foot the toe.

ParameterIn the pictureEffect

FACE_DEG YOUR valuethe dashed faceGround falling steeper than this is embankment; gentler is the natural slope. Prairie slopes are usually under 5 degrees.

CREST_FT, RING_FT YOUR valuethe search distancesHow far from the water the rim is looked for, and how far the face is followed to its toe. Raise RING when the notes say the face is still falling at the edge.
DEPTH_FT YOUR valuebelow the poolThe LiDAR cannot see under the water; the storage below the pool uses this greatest depth and the shoreline slope (as step 3).
RELEASE_AF YOUR value-Below this volume above the natural ground the pond is marked "no breach flood".

What the tool cannot tell: a man-made from a natural embankment (a road fill or a glacial ridge that holds water is an "embankment pond"), a steep natural kettle from a dugout, an old dugout with slumped banks from a depression. Check a township's classes on an aerial image before a county run.

2. Where the breach goes for a DWR dam (step 4)

The dam point is matched to its DWR dam line (structure ID first, then distance and name). The side away from the reservoir is downstream (the terrain is the cross-check); the breach goes where the ground at the downstream toe is lowest along the line - the tallest section. Without a dam line: flow away from the reservoir centroid, dir_conf = low, listed on the Review tab. MATCH_FT is the farthest a dam point may be from its line; ARROW_FT only draws the arrow.

3. The quick screen (step 6)

A screening shortcut: a dam whose generous envelope reaches no habitable structure, no road that could be Medium or High, no railroad, lifeline or other dam is cleared without a 2D run. Keep the audit share; the random audit is the check on the shortcut. BIG always sends large reservoirs to the 2D model.

4. The class (step 8): ND 5.2 / 5.3 as the shared rules module applies them

The chart method needs the digitized ACER TM 11 chart file (ND Dam Hazard's chart sign-off). The 2 ft depth rule is allowed by the policy for a 1D model only; these floods are 2D, so the plugin warns when it is chosen. Everything above is a screening suggestion that ranks the dams for review.

5. Units

Every distance and depth the engineer sees is in feet, areas in acres, volumes in acre-feet, flows in cfs. The one exception is the cell size of a grid, which is a property of the DEM (3DEP comes as 1 m, 2 m or 1/3 arc-second) and stays in metres, with the feet shown in its label. The tools compute in metres (the DEMs and EPSG:5070 are metric) and convert once at the parameter boundary; the output layers carry both (height_m and height_ft).
