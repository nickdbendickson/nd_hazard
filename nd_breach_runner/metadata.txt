    3.26.9 - With ND Dam Hazard 2.34.5 and ND Dam Breach Screening 0.32.4. Build a HEC-RAS 2D model: a dam point off the embankment (the dam.json point on the pool, away from the crest) is moved to the embankment crest found from the nearest crest-band ground within 3 toe distances (at least 300 ft). The move is made only when the crest passes the crest test (the ridge stands above the ground on both sides), its normal is within 75 degrees of the flow direction, and the dam point is upstream or downstream of it (within 45 degrees of its normal). When the move is rejected, the dam line stays through the dam point and the README says why. If the model cannot be built along the moved dam line, it is built with the line through the dam point with a note. A reservoir that simplifies to nothing (one cell wide) keeps its unsimplified outline and an empty polygon is skipped. LISFLOOD-FP and ANUGA runs are unchanged.
    3.26.8 - With ND Dam Hazard 2.34.5 and ND Dam Breach Screening 0.32.4. Build a HEC-RAS 2D model: cells that the 2D perimeter cuts in two are dealt with before the model is written. HEC-RAS 6.6 stopped the Absaraka (DWR-2801) model with roads and culverts with '1 face errors detected on the perimeter of the mesh': a grid point beside a narrow notch of the perimeter (a dry strip a little wider than twice the side buffer) had a cell split into two pieces. Every cell that reaches the perimeter is now checked (against the perimeter made valid where it crosses itself). An export with no such cell (and no grid point that close to the perimeter, below) is written as before. When there are some, their points are removed in repeated passes (up to 25: the neighbours that take a removed point's area can be cut in two in turn; when the removals spread instead of dying out, the points of the pass that left fewest such cells are kept), then each cell along the perimeter still cut in two or with more than 8 faces is mended where that leaves fewer such cells over the whole 2D area (a removed point put back, its point removed or moved to the middle of its cell, or a point added in the cell or next to it, at least 0.2 cell from the perimeter and a quarter cell from the other points). The 2D area is also drawn a second time with its notches narrower than 1.2 cells closed where those cells are, and so that its perimeter does not cross itself (that adds dry ground at the edge, there only; closing a gap between parts of the flood can also bring in a part that was left out), its points removed and mended the same way. The better of the two is written, ranked by: fewer problems that stop HEC-RAS (cells cut in two, cells of more than 8 faces, points on, outside or within 0.05 cell of the perimeter, the boundary line crossing the perimeter, a perimeter that crosses itself); then less flooded ground (Depth (Max) above 0, on the run's own cells) left outside the 2D area, differences within 0.05% of the flooded area below the dam being a tie; then a dam line whose ends and length move no more than a quarter cell; then fewer points removed; the one as drawn when none differs. The second one is never written when it leaves more than 0.5% of the flooded area more outside than the 2D area as drawn. The README says which was written and why, how much larger it is and how much flooded ground it takes in. Nor is a 2D area written that has more of those problems than the 2D area as drawn with its grid points as laid before 3.26.8, with none removed or with those whose cell is cut in two removed once (as this version first did): the better of those is written instead, with a note. Any problems left get a WARNING in the README naming the points (move or delete them in RAS Mapper, or use another cell size); the model is still written, and the README's count of cells of more than 8 faces is then the WARNING's (it also counts the cells the pre-check found farther in). Grid points closer than 0.05 cell to the perimeter (a notch narrower than a cell running between a point and its neighbours, which the 0.3 cell rule missed) are no longer laid. The DamSafety Runner's perimeter pre-check (3.10.22) also checks sliver faces and corners and cells of more than 8 faces, and stops the build. LISFLOOD-FP and ANUGA runs are unchanged. Build a HEC-RAS 2D model, culverts the run cut as gaps from the crossings (CROSSING_GAPS): where the run finds the channel on both sides of the road, the barrel now runs from where the channel leaves the road embankment on one side to where it leaves it on the other, beyond the outermost carriageway the crossing line meets (both carriageways of a divided highway). The run looks along the toe of the embankment in its terrain, every grid cell up to 40-100 m either side of the crossing point and out to 150 m from the road; the channel is the valley of those toes (at least 1 ft below the toes on both sides of it) nearest the crossing point, so that an NBI point off the channel, a channel crossing the road at an angle (up to 60 degrees: the barrel is then skewed, its upstream and downstream centreline stations apart) or bending at the toe, and a fill over 10 m high still get the channel. A side has no channel where its toes have no such valley (a field, a cross-slope along the road, a ditch along the toe), or where next to the valley the terrain has no data before the toe (with 10 m to spare), the ground is still falling more than 1 ft in 10 m out to 150 m (a steep channel), or another road meeting or crossing this one, or a bend of this one (at more than 30 degrees to it), comes within 10 m of the ground from the carriageway to 10 m beyond the toe (its fill can cover the channel). Each end is moved onto the lowest pixel of the model's terrain within 5 m (and an invert the run found there lowered to that pixel where it is lower: the run reads its terrain at 3 m). Upstream is the side the run's flow path comes from (where it passes within 1,000 ft of the culvert and crosses the road at 14.5 degrees or more), else the side with the higher channel bed at the toe (a WARNING with both beds), else the end the run's crossing line starts from (a WARNING); a note says where the beds disagree with the flow path. The inverts are the inventory's as the run used them where it gives them, each at its own (upstream or downstream) end, an adverse slope noted, else the channel bed at each end ('channel bed beside the road', not an inventory elevation). HEC-RAS centres a barrel on its connection line, so the line runs along the road over the point of a carriageway's crest (within 1 ft of the highest; the downstream carriageway of a divided highway, the downstream edge of a flat top) that puts the upstream end nearest the upstream toe; the barrel is long enough to reach both ends from there and at least one cell, it is lengthened half a cell at a time (up to 2 cells) until both ends of each barrel are in different cells, each end sliding up to 5 m along the road onto the cell that reaches lowest, and further (up to 1 cell, or 4 terrain pixels where that is more) only to the shortest length that brings the cells at its ends at least 0.25 ft nearer the inverts; an invert below the lowest ground of the 2D cell at its end + 0.05 ft is raised to that, the lowest ground being the higher of the terrain read between its pixels and the pixels whose centres lie in the cell (as the DamSafety Runner's fit_to_cells does, 3.10.56, with the pixel reading added). Where the channel is not found on both sides (or the run was made before 3.26.8) or the barrel cannot be exported, the culvert is exported exactly as before 3.26.8 (the run's 120 m line, turned to start upstream when the run's flow path tells, its inverts the run's or the terrain at the line's ends, left out where it was left out before), with a WARNING that says why; where both ends of a barrel fall in one 2D cell it is lengthened half a cell at a time (up to 2 cells), an invert below its cell + 0.05 ft is raised (a WARNING), and it is left out, with a WARNING, where its ends cannot be put in two cells. A culvert the export left out before (no terrain at an end of the 120 m line, or the line outside the 2D area) that now gets its barrel carries a WARNING that says so, and an invert more than 0.5 ft above the channel bed found at its end (raised to a 2D cell that misses the channel, the inventory's invert after the run's LiDAR check, or the ground at the end of the run's line) is a WARNING with both elevations. Each change is a note in the README. It was the whole 120 m gap line in an arbitrary direction with the terrain at its ends as inverts, and HEC-RAS passed no water through Absaraka's NBI 9117181 (inlet on a field 10 ft above the channel). LISFLOOD-FP, the built-in engine and ANUGA cut these crossings exactly as 3.26.7 did (the whole gap line, with the inventory's inverts only), so their runs are unchanged and a 3.26.7 checkpoint still resumes.
    3.26.7 - With ND Dam Hazard 2.34.5 and ND Dam Breach Screening 0.32.4. Crossings: the NBI layer is read as the NBI writes it. The offline pack's NBI_bridges layer keeps the NBI's own field names, and none of them matched, so every NBI culvert was an unnamed bridge with no span. Now STRUCTURE_NUMBER_008 is the name, STRUCTURE_TYPE_043B gives the kind (19 = culvert, any other code a bridge), MAX_SPAN_LEN_MT_048 is the span (metres, converted to feet; any span or rise field named ..._MT_... or ..._M is metres) and MAIN_UNIT_SPANS_045 the barrels. The NBI has no culvert rise, so an NBI culvert is still only reported unless you give a rise to assume (new Crossings: rise to assume, CROSSING_RISE_FT, ft, 0 = off): with it and 'cut ... as gaps' on, those culverts are cut too, the rise never deeper than the embankment the terrain shows, and the run report marks the rise as assumed. Culvert candidates (roads across the flow path) now name the inventory crossing within 75 m (name, kind, span, barrels) in culvert_candidates.csv and in the HEC-RAS export's GIS/culvert_candidates.csv, as the DamSafety Runner's culvert guess matching does; nothing is put in the model from that. With the defaults a run is unchanged except for the crossing names, kinds and spans in the report.
    3.26.6 - With ND Dam Hazard 2.34.5 and ND Dam Breach Screening 0.32.4. Build a HEC-RAS 2D model: fixes found by opening and running the model in real HEC-RAS 6.6 (Ras.exe under Wine with .NET 4.8, Absaraka DWR-2801), the first time a Runner model was run in HEC-RAS. (1) The normal-depth boundary line is drawn a quarter cell outside the 2D perimeter: on the perimeter HEC-RAS stopped with 'BC Line intersects the perimeter ... must be entirely outside'. (2) Finer cells below the breach (a quarter and a half of the 2D cell, no finer than a third of the breach bottom width, within about 500 and 1,250 ft of the breach centre) and HEC-RAS's adaptive (Courant) time step from the finest cells: with one cell size the breach opened into one or two 100 ft cells and HEC-RAS hit its iteration limit every step there, the breach flow flipping sign. (3) HEC-RAS stopped with 'weir breach lower than the cells they are connected to' / 'weir elevation lower than the cells': with the dam line on the crest the 2D cells next to the breach sit on the embankment. As in the DamSafety Runner, the breach bottom is no lower than the ground at the toe (HEC-RAS cannot drain the pool lower; on DWR-2801 1035.7 -> 1047.1 ft) and the breach opening is cut into the terrain GeoTIFF down to it through the downstream half of the embankment; any dam weir point or breach bottom still below the lowest ground of a 2D cell next to it is raised to it. (4) A dam point off the embankment (DWR-2801's dam.json point is on the pool, 92 ft from the crest) put the dam line through the reservoir, across the direction to the run's inflow point, and HEC-RAS drained the breach into a closed pocket of the pool: when the ground at the dam point is more than max(3 ft, 10% of the dam height) below the top of dam, the dam line now goes through the nearest ground within that of the top of dam (within 3 toe distances, at least 300 ft), along the long axis of that high ground, and the reservoir is seeded just behind it. (5) The reservoir's elevation-volume rows are written only while both written values rise: HEC-RAS stopped the synthetic test dam with 'Volume Elevation relationship does not have increasing volume' because the pool bottom wrote 0.00 acre-ft three times. Each change is a note in the README. LISFLOOD-FP and ANUGA runs are unchanged. (6) Culvert connections inside the 2D area are 'Conn Routing Type= 1' (weir, gates, culverts), as DamSafety Runner 3.10.56 found: type 3 made HEC-RAS 6.6 read the culvert as a rating-curve family with no curves, and stop.
    3.26.5 - The water leaving the model also extends the domain. The domain used to grow only while an edge was deeper than the edge tolerance (1 ft), so a wide sheet under 1 ft could carry a large share of the breach volume out of the model with the domain never growing there (36-40% at Absaraka, DWR-2801, with Runner defaults). Now, once 5% of the breach volume has left (new advanced setting OUTFLOW_EXTEND_PCT, outflow_extend_pct in the CLI JSON; 0 = the depth rule alone, as before), every edge that carries at least 10% of the leaving water (estimated from the max depths along the edges, depth^(5/3)) and is wet past the arrival depth moves out too; the run report's extent table lists it under Over and run_report.json under edges_over_outflow. The 1 ft edge tolerance itself is unchanged: on Raleigh, Elm River 1 and Swan Buffalo 8 a 0.25 ft tolerance changed little (runs-notes/edge-rule-extent-2026-10-26). The outflow warning now says why the run stopped instead of blaming the depth rule, and a tolerance such as 0.25 ft prints as 0.25 ft, not 0.2 ft. The setting is in the run key, so older checkpoints are not resumed.
    3.26.3 - With ND Dam Hazard 2.34.4 and ND Dam Breach Screening 0.32.4. Arrival depth (ARRIVAL_FT) now defaults to 0.1 ft, the ND hazard wet depth, instead of 1 ft, so every wet structure gets an arrival time (REG_05.2026 5.4(c)); same as DWR_Plugins Runner 3.10.53 (#131, numbered 3.10.45 on its branch). One constant, core/units.ARRIVAL_DEPTH_FT, feeds RunSettings, the Run a dam tool, the HEC-RAS import tool, hecras.read_mesh / import_results / terrain_depth, Breach products from rasters, the CLI JSON template and hecras-reexport --arrival-depth-ft, and the LISFLOOD-FP and ANUGA settings defaults. The same depth is the run summary's flooded depth (flooded_acres_over_arrival_depth, receptors flooded), so flooded areas, receptor counts and arrival-zone polygons grow on a re-run. The domain-extension edge tolerance stays 1 ft, but the LISFLOOD-FP domain loop's 'still advancing' test (latest arrival after 90% of the simulated time) uses the arrival depth, so the thin 0.1 ft front keeps it going more often than 1 ft did: the next run is 1.5x longer (automatic, or a chosen duration up to DURATION_MAX_HR), so runs take longer, and a run that cannot lengthen ends with a 'still advancing' Results cut off note. arrival_depth_ft is in the run key, so 1 ft checkpoints are not resumed. Results made before 3.26.3 used 1 ft: re-run or re-export them for arrival times under 1 ft.

    3.13.0 - With ND Dam Hazard 2.23.0 and ND Dam Breach Screening 0.21.0 (damsuite 1.9.0). Ideas from the DamSafety plugins audit (2026-10-08). Pre-run check before the flood solve: the DEM covers the dam point (and not mostly no-data around it), the breach hydrograph has a peak and a volume (a 2-point curve is a warning), and LISFLOOD-FP runs 1 s on a 5 x 5 grid (once per executable; not for the built-in engine; ND_DAM_PRE_RUN_SMOKE=0 skips it); a failure stops the run in seconds with the reason, and the report lists the checks (pre_run_check). A structures or culvert layer whose whole extent misses the study area stops the run and says whether it has no CRS or simply does not overlap (a statewide layer passes). The DWR dam line is used as the crest: CREST_LINE fills from the dam folder (dwr_dam_lines), the line with the dam's DWR structure id wins (else the nearest within 500 m), and a dam point off the line is moved onto it (logged; a BREACH_POSITION you give is kept). Breach inflow fallback: when no cell at least half the dam height down lies near the dam point, the nearest such cell within 400 m is used; when the top of dam is known and that also fails, the dam point moves to the highest ground within 400 m that is near the top of dam and the search runs from there; each fallback is a warning in the report (dam_xy_given). The first search is unchanged, so earlier runs give the same results. Culvert candidates: every place a road crosses the downstream flow path that has no inventory structure within 30 m is listed in the report, culvert_candidates.csv and the HEC-RAS model folder (GIS/culvert_candidates.csv, README_HECRAS.md); they are never put in the model. Suite terrain cache: a tile whose download fails is asked for again in four quarters (the cache grid is unchanged). New menu item Load the dam's data layers: the dam folder's data and the suite DWR dams / dam lines in one layer group, so you see what a run will use. The wizard starts on the suite's DWR dam inventory, lists each dam as ID, name, height and storage (DWR-<structure id> when there is no NID ID) and reads DWR max_pool_vol / max_pool_area. See README.md.

    3.11.0: With ND Dam Hazard 2.19.0 and ND Dam Breach Screening 0.18.0. Set up ANUGA without conda: a one-time tool (Checks tab, Plugins menu, ndbreach:setupanuga, ndbreach_cli.py setup-anuga) downloads micromamba (SHA-256 checked) and installs ANUGA from conda-forge into a private environment in the plugin data folder; the Runner finds it on its own (about 360 MB download, 1.6 GB on disk; not on ARM Windows or Linux). Works without installing LISFLOOD-FP: a patched LISFLOOD-FP is bundled for Windows 64-bit and Linux 64-bit (bin/, with its source and the GPL-2 text), and a built-in flood engine (numpy, same local-inertial method, same input files) runs on any computer; choose it with the new provider setting "Use the built-in flood engine" or the word builtin as the LISFLOOD-FP path. Fixes from the Elm River Detention Dam 1 run (see RUNNER_FV1_INSTABILITY.md). The near-dam fine run is checked before it is merged: water in the domain more than 10% above what was injected plus what was already ponded, boundary inflow after the flood, or a depth over twice the dam height reject it, and the ACC results are kept everywhere (the fine run used to be merged unchecked: max depth 39,137 ft). The fine run closes the upstream and side edges of its box (an edge not listed in the .bci is a wall) so it cannot import water through a free edge in the reservoir basin, and its clock follows the flood travel time to the box end instead of the main run duration. The dam line is raised to the top of dam in the model DEM (RAISE_CREST, on by default; the CREST_LINE layer, else a ridge traced from the DEM; the source DEM is not changed) so breach water does not spill back into the empty reservoir. DURATION_HR 0 = automatic as its help says, and the clock is lengthened while the flood is still moving. The resume key no longer includes the refine settings, so changing REFINE_NEAR reuses finished runs. The run report gains the late boundary inflow, the water in the domain against the injected volume, and the rejected-fine-run section. See README.md.
